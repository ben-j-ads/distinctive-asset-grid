How it works

The measures
Fame = named you / shown the asset Uniqueness = named you / named any brand Score = 100 x Fame^w x Uniqueness^(1 - w) (w = 0.5 by default)

The geometric mean is deliberate: an asset that is famous but shared, or unique but unknown, scores low. Fame and uniqueness have Wilson 95% ranges. The score range and the "sure of the box" figure come from 4,000 seeded draws from a Dirichlet posterior over three outcomes: linked to you, linked to a rival, linked to nobody.

Bands: 70 and above is ownable, 45 to 69 building, 20 to 44 weak, below 20 not an asset. These cut-offs are conventions.

The four boxes

Use or lose: famous and yours. Protect it and put it in every high-reach touchpoint. Invest: yours alone but not famous yet, so it needs reach and repetition. Avoid solo use: famous but shared with rivals, so on its own it advertises the category. Test or ignore: neither. Rework it or spend the effort elsewhere.

Same method as the assetscan app

This is a browser version of the assetscan scoring. The fame, uniqueness, score, box and Wilson ranges match it exactly. The score range and box probabilities are Monte Carlo draws, so they can differ from the Python app's by about a point. assetscan also handles segments and an execution audit.

Read this before you trust the grid

It scores the data you collect. It does not run the survey. Use unbranded stimuli, category buyers and unprompted recall.

Prompted tests inflate fame. Showing a brand list can raise it by up to about 20 points in Romaniuk's work. Treat prompted fame as a ceiling.

The lines and bands are conventions. Change them in Settings and see how much the boxes move.

The score is a ranking aid. It has not been tested against sales.
